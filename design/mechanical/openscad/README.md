## Board layout on the rear plate (decided 20 November 2026)

The owner left the internal layout to the design. **Pi and HAT side by side does not fit this case**: the inner width is 151.18 mm, while a Pi 4 with USB/Ethernet overhang and inserted SD card (≈ 90.5 mm) plus the Louder HAT Plus 1X (61.38 mm) already needs ≈ 151.9 mm before any clearance. They are therefore **stacked**, as a HAT normally sits on the Pi, on one shared 58 × 49 mm M2.5 pattern.

| Item | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Pattern | 4 × M2.5 tapped through holes, plate coordinates X 73.68 / 131.68, Y 28.09 / 77.09 |
| Orientation | Pi USB/Ethernet end toward the side wall (+X seen from outside); GPIO edge **down** toward the I/O cap; HAT connector edge (DC jack + speaker screw terminal) up toward the top wall |
| Order from the plate | 4 mm standoff (room for a thermal pad) · HAT with its flat back to the plate · Pi, underside toward the screen |
| Depth | ≈ 27.4 mm from the plate's inner face: 4 + 20 (Louder's listed HAT height) + Pi 1.4 + underside 2 |
| Clearances (nominal) | 2.0 mm Pi ports to side wall; HAT 1.0 mm above the I/O cap; Pi top edge 2.6 mm below the button pins; 8.2 mm between HAT connector edge and top wall |
| Free for PD module + speaker terminals | 38.8 mm of the cap ceiling beside the stack |
| Free for INA228, TSR/ULN carrier, fuse, WAGOs | 58.7 mm wide side zone beside the stack |
| Airflow | stack between the left bottom opening and the top vent slot |
| Pi 4 heat | The SoC is on the Pi's component side, facing the HAT's inductor side, ≈ 10 mm apart. A Pi 4 can throttle there. Check `vcgencmd measure_temp` and `vcgencmd get_throttled` under load in the closed case; a low heatsink on the SoC is possible only where it clears the HAT's inductors (check on the boards) |
| Pi 4 ports | 2 × micro-HDMI and USB-C power on the long edge facing the top wall; Ethernet on the GPIO side of the port end. Service only, like USB-A; the HAT powers the Pi through the GPIO header |
| Ethernet / USB-A (owner: service only) | The Pi's USB/Ethernet end faces the side wall with 2 mm to spare: no plug fits with the case closed. Normal use is Wi-Fi; with the rear plate removed the ports are free, because the stack comes out with the plate |
| Wi-Fi | The Pi's antenna sits at the SD-card end, under the HAT, between the HAT's copper and the LCD. Radiation leaves through the wooden walls. Not verified: measure the signal with plate, HAT and screen fitted (`docs/design/wall-mechanical-c1.md`). Keep metal parts away from the stack's SD-card end |

Why the GPIO edge points down: with it up, the HAT's DC jack faced the I/O cap with 0.8 mm to spare (no plug or soldered lead fits) and the Pi's top edge overlapped the button pins in height, leaving only ≈ 0.3 mm in depth. With it down, the button keeps 2.6 mm and the jack edge has 8.2 mm. That is still too little for a barrel plug: **the 20 V lead to the HAT is soldered to the jack** (owner agreed). The speaker leads go from the HAT's screw terminal to the terminals in the I/O cap; use flexible silicone wire bent right at the terminal, as 8.2 mm leaves no room for long ferrules.

HAT facts from the Louder Rev F production files (same SHA-256 source): the GPIO socket is SMD on the component side (no 40-pin through holes; 140 paste pads on top, 4 below), so the inductors face the Pi and the flat back faces the plate. The edge opposite the GPIO socket carries the DC-044A jack (2.5 mm pin) and a 4-pole 5.0 mm screw terminal (Ø1.7 mm holes) centred 8.2 mm from that edge. 162 Ø0.3 mm vias spread amplifier heat to the back copper, which now faces the aluminium plate.

Board data: Louder Rev F manufacturer STEP (65.00 × 61.38 mm PCB, holes 3.5 mm from the ends, 4.09 mm from the GPIO edge, tallest part 7.7 mm) and Adafruit 3350 button STEP, both matching the SHA-256 in `docs/design/fit-d1-sources.json`; Pi 4 Model B (owner, 20 Nov 2026) 85 × 56 mm with the standard hole pattern; the same footprint as a Pi 3B+, so the plate fits either. `show_stack = true` in `backplate.scad` shows the board envelopes.

**Screen (owner, 20 Nov 2026): the LCD panel is taped with its back onto the wooden front face.** Checked against the Waveshare 7inch DSI LCD (E) STEP (2025-04-28, same SHA-256 as `docs/design/fit-d1-sources.json`). By depth from the glass front: cover glass 170.18 × 104.20 × 0.75; LCD panel 161.83 × 99.80 up to 5.0 mm, 2.96 mm off-centre; driver board, flex and standoffs at most 138.6 × 76.0, down to 15.3 mm. The panel is larger than the 151.18 × 85.2 mm opening, so it rests on the wood; the rear part fits the opening (1.0 mm to the side wall at its tightest). Glass + panel + 0.5 mm tape stand **5.5 mm** proud. No overlap with the wood, the button or the Pi/HAT envelope; **9.8 mm** remain between the back of the screen and the Pi.

`bezel-ring.scad` closes the proud band: an anodised ring (6 mm stock, 174.8 × 108.8 mm outer, 2 mm visible rim) around the off-centre panel, with a front pocket that holds the glass flush. It overhangs the case by ≈ 2.8 mm all round. Walls beside the panel ≈ 3.2 / 9.1 mm (sides) and 4.2 mm (top/bottom); its contact with the wood is small (0.4 / 6.3 / 1.4 mm), so bond it mainly to the panel and glass edges. The display's flex tail lies 0.1 mm behind the panel back over the wood: keep the tape ≥ 0.5 mm there. Corner reliefs let square glass and panel corners seat.

**Depth budget (superseded by the screen choice above).** Case 47 mm deep, screen module 15.3 mm (Waveshare STEP envelope incl. its connectors, see `docs/design/fit-d1.md`) → 31.7 mm behind the screen if the glass sits at the front face; the stack takes ≈ 27.4 mm, leaving ≈ 4.3 mm for the DSI flex and air. The supplied case is 169.18 × 103.20 mm while the glass is 170.18 × 104.20 mm, and the owner confirms the glass lies on the front face. The depth budget above therefore holds; confirm the module depth on the real screen before ordering.
